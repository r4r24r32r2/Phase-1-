V-taper focus

Build the V

A V-taper means wide shoulders and a wide back above a narrow waist. The plan now leans that way.

  • Wider shoulders. Lateral raises are 4 sets on Monday and Friday. Press movements add the front and top of the shoulder.
  • Wider back. Wide-grip pull-ups, rows and pullovers on Tuesday and Friday train the lats, the muscles that create the V shape.
  • Fuller upper chest. Feet-elevated push-ups and pike push-ups fill out the top of the torso.
  • Narrow waist. Core work uses holds and stomach vacuums instead of weighted twists or side bends, which can thicken the waist. Keep weight gain slow, about 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week, so it goes to muscle and not belly.
  • Posture. Shoulders back and chest tall make the V look bigger. The rear-shoulder raises and wall angels help with that.

Bone structure sets the limit on shoulder and hip width, and training cannot change it. Clearly bigger shoulders and lats usually take 6 to 12 months or more of steady work.

When to make it harder

Five and 7.5 kg are light. You build muscle with them by going slow, going to near failure and changing the exercise as you get stronger. Use this rule on every exercise.

  1. Hit the top of the rep range on every set. For 3 × 12 to 15, that means 15, 15, 15 with clean form.
  2. Do it in two sessions in a row. One good day can be luck.
  3. Change one thing. Slow the lowering to 4 seconds, add a 2-second pause, move to a harder version, or add weight.

12-week roadmap

  1. Weeks 1 to 2
    Learn the movesDo 3 sets of everything and stop 3 reps before you fail. Form matters more than numbers.
  2. Weeks 3 to 5
    Build volumeAdd the fourth set where it is listed. Push towards the top of each rep range.
  3. Week 6
    Light weekDo 2 sets of each exercise. Your joints and nervous system get a break, then you come back stronger.
  4. Weeks 7 to 11
    Level upUse harder versions, pauses and a loaded backpack. This is when the dumbbells alone start to feel light.
  5. Week 12
    Test and resetRecord max push-ups, squats and pull-ups, then plan the next block from the numbers.

What to expect

Strength and form improve in the first 3 to 4 weeks. Shoulders, arms and chest usually start to look different around weeks 8 to 12 if food and sleep are on track. A clearly more athletic build takes 6 to 12 months.

The physiques in your reference photos usually come from years of training, very low body fat, genetics and good lighting. Use them for direction, not as a deadline.

Gear that helps

  • A loaded backpack with books for squats, rows and push-ups. Free, and you can add weight a little at a time.
  • A doorway pull-up bar. Pulling is the hardest thing to train with light dumbbells.
  • A set of resistance bands, then adjustable dumbbells or weight plates by week 6 to 8.

Weekly check-in

Once a week, same scale, same time, before breakfast. Weight should go up about 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week. Your starting weight is already filled in.

Eat to grow

At 55 kg and about 5 ft 7 in, your body needs more food than you are probably eating to add muscle. Training without eating enough stalls progress. These are starting estimates, and a doctor or registered dietitian can tune them to you.

If your weight has not moved after 3 weeks, add about 200 kcal a day, for example a glass of milk with a banana. Aim for steady gain, not fast gain.

A sample day

Keep it simple

  • Food first. Skip pre-workout powders and fat burners.
  • Eat protein at every meal, not just dinner.
  • Rest days need full meals too, because that is when you grow.
  • Weigh in once a week. Daily weighing makes normal ups and downs feel like problems.

Getting comfortable with a gym

You do not need a gym for this plan. If you want to try one later, take it one small step at a time. Tick each step when you are ready to move on.

Things that help

  • Most people in a gym are focused on their own sets, not on you.
  • Keep the plan on your phone so you never stand around wondering what is next.
  • Headphones and a quiet time slot make a big difference.
  • Stepping back one rung is fine. It is not failing.
  • If worry about being watched is affecting school, friends or sleep, a school counselor or doctor can help. It is common and it gets better with support.

Train safely

  • See the family doctor before starting if there is any history of heart problems, asthma, fainting or joint injuries.
  • Learn the form before adding weight. Filming one set from the side shows your back and hips.
  • Muscle burn is normal. Sharp pain or joint pain means stop that exercise and swap it.
  • Never skip the warm-up. It takes 8 minutes and protects your shoulders, knees and back.
  • Leave 1 to 3 reps in reserve on most sets. Max-effort single lifts are not for this age.
  • Use only sturdy furniture for step-ups, split squats and rows.
  • Skip training if you are ill or have a fever, and rest.
  • Never use steroids, SARMs or testosterone boosters. They are dangerous at 16 and unnecessary.

This plan is general guidance and is not medical advice.
